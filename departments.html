OUR ORGANIZATION

Departments

LifeLab's departments work together to support the organization's programs, operations and day-to-day activities. Each unit has a defined role while maintaining close coordination with the others to ensure that programs are planned, implemented, monitored and supported effectively.

This internal structure brings together program management, monitoring and learning, finance, human resources, procurement, logistics, information management and communications. Through this coordination, LifeLab seeks to maintain organized processes, responsible use of resources and consistent support for its activities and communities.

Operations, Programs & Strategies (OPS)

The Operations, Programs and Strategies unit oversees the coordination and implementation of LifeLab's programs and activities. It helps connect strategic objectives with practical implementation and works across departments to maintain coordination between programs, operational requirements and organizational priorities.

The unit supports program planning from the early stages of an initiative through implementation and follow-up. This includes contributing to project design, defining activities, identifying operational requirements and coordinating with the departments involved in delivery.

OPS also contributes to research and organizational development by reviewing processes, identifying areas that can be improved and supporting the development of approaches that respond to changing community needs and program priorities.

Key responsibilities

  • Program and activity planning.
  • Coordination between departments and projects.
  • Support for project and proposal development.
  • Initial planning of program resources and budgets.
  • Follow-up on program implementation.
  • Research and development of internal processes.
  • Coordination of program priorities and operational needs.

Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability & Learning (MEAL)

The MEAL unit supports LifeLab in understanding whether its programs are being implemented as intended and whether they are producing meaningful results. It develops systems for monitoring activities, measuring progress and documenting outputs and outcomes throughout the program cycle.

Monitoring provides regular information on activities and implementation, while evaluation helps assess the relevance and results of interventions. Accountability mechanisms provide participants and communities with opportunities to communicate feedback, concerns and suggestions related to the programs that affect them.

Learning is equally important. Information collected through monitoring, evaluation and feedback can be used to identify lessons, improve future activities and strengthen organizational decision-making.

Key responsibilities

  • Development of monitoring and evaluation systems.
  • Collection and organization of program data.
  • Tracking program outputs and outcomes.
  • Supporting assessments and evaluations.
  • Accountability and community feedback mechanisms.
  • Program reporting and documentation.
  • Identification and documentation of lessons learned.

Finance

The Finance department manages the organization's financial processes and resources. Its role is to help ensure that funds are planned, recorded and managed responsibly while maintaining appropriate financial controls and documentation.

Finance works closely with program and operational teams to support budgeting and expenditure monitoring. Clear financial procedures allow the organization to understand how resources are being used and support responsible financial decision-making.

The department also contributes to financial reporting, accounting, audit preparation, cash-flow management and compliance with applicable requirements and internal procedures.

Key responsibilities

  • Budget preparation and financial planning.
  • Accounting and financial record keeping.
  • Monitoring expenditures against approved budgets.
  • Cash-flow and payment management.
  • Financial reporting.
  • Internal financial controls.
  • Audit preparation and financial documentation.
  • Support for financial compliance.

Human Resources

The Human Resources department supports the people responsible for carrying out LifeLab's work. It manages processes related to recruitment, staff administration, professional development, workplace policies and employee relations.

Building an effective organization requires people with appropriate skills and clearly defined responsibilities. Human Resources therefore supports recruitment and onboarding while also helping departments identify training and development needs.

The department contributes to maintaining an organized, respectful and safe working environment through performance management, staff engagement, conflict resolution and the application of internal policies and procedures.

Key responsibilities

  • Recruitment and staffing.
  • Employee onboarding and personnel administration.
  • Performance management.
  • Training and professional development.
  • Employee engagement and relations.
  • Conflict resolution.
  • Workplace health and safety support.
  • Workforce and succession planning.

Procurement

The Procurement department supports LifeLab's activities by coordinating the acquisition of goods and services required for programs and organizational operations. Procurement processes should balance quality, cost, timing, transparency and operational requirements.

The department works with internal teams to understand purchasing needs, identify suitable suppliers, obtain quotations and support the selection of goods and services according to established procedures.

Maintaining clear procurement records and consistent processes is important for accountability and transparency. Supplier management and procurement planning also help ensure that required resources are available when activities need them.

Key responsibilities

  • Procurement planning.
  • Sourcing of goods and services.
  • Supplier and vendor management.
  • Quotation and cost comparison.
  • Purchasing documentation.
  • Risk and compliance considerations.
  • Coordination with finance, logistics and program teams.
  • Support for transparent and responsible purchasing.

Logistics

The Logistics department supports the movement, storage and availability of the resources required for LifeLab's activities. Effective logistics helps ensure that materials and supplies reach the appropriate location when they are needed.

Its responsibilities can include inventory control, warehousing, transportation and distribution planning. Logistics works closely with procurement and program teams to coordinate the flow of supplies from acquisition through storage and eventual use or distribution.

Logistics is particularly important during activities involving multiple locations or urgent community needs, where timely coordination and accurate information are necessary for an effective response.

Key responsibilities

  • Inventory and stock management.
  • Warehousing and storage coordination.
  • Transportation planning.
  • Distribution of program materials and supplies.
  • Supply-chain coordination.
  • Asset and equipment follow-up.
  • Logistical support for field activities.
  • Emergency logistics planning where required.

Information Management & Technology

The Information Management and Technology unit supports the systems, information and digital tools used across LifeLab. Reliable technology and organized information are important for communication, program management, reporting and daily organizational operations.

The unit helps maintain the organization's technology environment and provides technical support to staff. It also contributes to responsible management of digital information by supporting appropriate access, data organization, security and privacy practices.

As programs increasingly depend on digital systems, information management also supports the effective use of data for coordination, monitoring and decision-making.

Key responsibilities

  • IT infrastructure and network support.
  • Management and maintenance of digital systems.
  • User technical support.
  • Data organization and information management.
  • Data security and privacy practices.
  • Technology maintenance and troubleshooting.
  • Staff support and basic technology training.
  • Planning for organizational technology needs.

Media & Communications

The Media and Communications department manages how LifeLab communicates its work, activities and messages to communities, partners and the wider public. Clear communication helps people understand the organization's areas of work and remain informed about relevant programs and activities.

The department supports digital communication, social media, activity coverage, public information and communication materials. It also works with program teams to document activities and present information in a clear and consistent manner.

Communication is not limited to visibility. It also supports community engagement by helping relevant information reach the people who may need it and by creating accessible ways to communicate programs, opportunities and organizational updates.

Key responsibilities

  • External and public communications.
  • Social media management.
  • Website and digital content.
  • Documentation and coverage of activities.
  • Development of communication materials.
  • Media outreach and coordination.
  • Event and campaign communication.
  • Internal communication support.
