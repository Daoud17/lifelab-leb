OUR SECTORS

Areas of Work

LifeLab works across interconnected sectors that influence people's quality of life, opportunities and ability to build sustainable futures. Our work brings together health, education, livelihoods, agriculture, empowerment, protection and community development.

These sectors are not treated as separate challenges. Education influences employment, employment affects food security and household well-being, health affects people's ability to learn and work, and strong communities depend on inclusion, participation and access to essential services. LifeLab therefore approaches development through practical, connected interventions shaped around people and their communities.

Food Security & Livelihoods

Access to food and the ability to earn a stable income are fundamental to living with dignity. Economic instability, unemployment, rising living costs and limited employment opportunities can place significant pressure on households and make it increasingly difficult for families to meet their basic needs.

LifeLab's work in food security and livelihoods focuses on strengthening people's capacity to earn, produce and participate in the economy. The objective is not only to respond to immediate economic hardship, but also to help individuals develop practical capabilities that can support greater independence and longer-term resilience.

Skills development can combine vocational competencies with life skills, financial literacy, communication, workplace readiness and entrepreneurship. Training should remain connected to realistic opportunities so that participants are developing skills that can be applied in employment, self-employment or productive activities.

Market-oriented livelihood development

Vocational and livelihood programs are most useful when they respond to actual economic conditions. Before selecting areas of training or business support, market analysis can be used to understand demand, identify gaps, assess competition and determine where viable opportunities may exist.

Relevant areas of analysis can include:

  • Local market conditions and economic activity.
  • Employment demand and skills gaps.
  • Industry and sector trends.
  • Customer needs and purchasing behavior.
  • Existing competitors and service providers.
  • Pricing and cost considerations.
  • Distribution and access to markets.
  • Potential opportunities for new businesses.

Based on identified opportunities, livelihood support can extend beyond training to business development, entrepreneurship, access to productive tools and stronger connections between participants and local markets.

Agriculture and rural livelihoods

Agriculture is closely connected to both food security and employment. Supporting farmers and agricultural workers can improve local production while creating income opportunities and strengthening the resilience of rural communities.

Areas of work can include modern irrigation practices, responsible water management, agricultural skills, sustainable production methods, agro-food activities and support that improves the connection between producers and markets.

Education

Education provides people with the knowledge, confidence and capabilities needed to improve their opportunities and participate more fully in society. LifeLab views access to meaningful learning as an important part of both individual development and poverty reduction.

Educational needs differ according to age, background and circumstance. Children may require support to remain engaged in school, while adolescents and young adults may need practical skills that help them transition toward further education, vocational training or work. Adults may also benefit from opportunities to develop literacy, digital, professional or technical competencies.

LifeLab's education approach can therefore combine formal-learning support with community-based and practical learning opportunities.

  • Basic literacy and numeracy support.
  • Learning and homework support.
  • Educational activities for children and adolescents.
  • Life-skills education.
  • Digital literacy and technology skills.
  • Vocational and technical training.
  • Career orientation and work-readiness activities.
  • Community awareness on the importance of education.
  • Learning opportunities for youth and adults.

The broader objective is to connect education with opportunity. Learning should equip participants not simply with information, but with capabilities that can improve their choices, participation and prospects for employment and economic independence.

Women Empowerment

Women's ability to access education, work, income and community participation has a direct effect on families and communities. Yet economic, social and structural barriers can continue to limit opportunities available to women.

LifeLab approaches women's empowerment as a combination of economic opportunity, skills development, participation, protection and personal capacity. Empowerment is not limited to a single training course; it involves creating conditions in which women can strengthen their capabilities and make informed decisions about their lives.

Economic empowerment

Access to livelihood opportunities can contribute to greater financial independence and household resilience. LifeLab seeks to support relevant technical and vocational skills, entrepreneurship and income-generating opportunities that respond to women's circumstances and local economic conditions.

Skills and participation

Life skills, communication, leadership and problem-solving can strengthen women's ability to participate confidently in professional and community environments. Community engagement can also create opportunities for women to contribute to initiatives and decisions affecting the places in which they live.

  • Vocational and employment-oriented skills.
  • Entrepreneurship and income-generation support.
  • Financial literacy.
  • Life and leadership skills.
  • Community participation.
  • Awareness and access to information.
  • Referral to appropriate support when required.

Youth Empowerment

Young people have an important role in Lebanon's social and economic future. However, the transition from education to employment can be difficult when young people lack practical experience, career guidance, professional networks or access to opportunities.

LifeLab seeks to help young people strengthen the capabilities needed to move from learning toward active participation in work and community life. Technical training is important, but it is most effective when combined with communication, teamwork, leadership, problem-solving and other transferable skills.

Youth empowerment also means giving young people opportunities to participate. Community activities, volunteering, youth-led initiatives and local engagement can allow them to develop experience while contributing directly to their communities.

  • Technical and vocational skills development.
  • Digital and technology-related competencies.
  • Employment readiness and career orientation.
  • Entrepreneurship and self-employment.
  • Communication, teamwork and leadership.
  • Youth-led community initiatives.
  • Volunteering and civic participation.
  • Connections to learning and work opportunities.

Social Stability & Good Governance

Strong communities depend on trust, participation and the ability to manage shared challenges constructively. Economic pressure, social exclusion and competition over limited resources can contribute to tensions and weaken relationships within communities.

LifeLab's social stability work focuses on dialogue, community participation and practical mechanisms that help people address common concerns. Activities can bring together community members, civil society and local actors to identify needs and cooperate around solutions.

Social cohesion

Building social cohesion requires opportunities for people to communicate and interact across differences. Conflict-resolution skills, dialogue, community activities and awareness initiatives can contribute to stronger relationships and more constructive ways of addressing disagreement.

Good governance

Good governance depends on participation, accountability, transparency and responsiveness. Communities should have meaningful opportunities to express their needs, while institutions and local actors need the capacity to communicate effectively and use available resources responsibly.

  • Community dialogue and engagement.
  • Conflict-resolution and mediation skills.
  • Civic awareness and participation.
  • Community needs assessments.
  • Capacity building for local actors.
  • Transparency and accountability awareness.
  • Participatory approaches to local development.
  • Community-led initiatives and cooperation.

WASH

Water, sanitation and hygiene are essential to health, dignity and safe living conditions. Limited access to clean water, adequate sanitation or basic hygiene resources can expose individuals and communities to preventable health risks.

LifeLab's WASH approach connects immediate needs with awareness and sustainable resource management. Depending on community needs, interventions can address water access, sanitation, hygiene practices and the efficient use of water resources.

WASH is also closely connected to LifeLab's work in health, environment and agriculture. Responsible water management can improve household well-being while supporting agricultural productivity and protecting increasingly limited natural resources.

  • Water, sanitation and hygiene awareness.
  • Promotion of safe hygiene practices.
  • Responsible water consumption.
  • Water conservation and management.
  • Community awareness activities.
  • Water-efficient agricultural practices.
  • Support for improved sanitation where needed.

Protection

Every person should be able to live safely and with dignity. Protection becomes particularly important for individuals and families facing displacement, poverty, violence, exploitation, discrimination or other forms of vulnerability.

LifeLab's protection approach emphasizes safe engagement, awareness, responsible identification of needs and connections to appropriate services. People experiencing protection concerns may require different forms of assistance, making coordinated referral and access to specialized support important.

Protection principles should also be integrated across education, humanitarian assistance, youth programs, women's empowerment and other community activities. Programs should be designed and delivered in ways that respect privacy, dignity, inclusion and safety.

  • Protection and rights awareness.
  • Safe community engagement.
  • Identification of protection concerns.
  • Referral to appropriate specialized services.
  • Support for vulnerable women, youth and families.
  • Prevention of exploitation and abuse.
  • Promotion of dignity and non-discrimination.

Health

Health is one of the central pillars of LifeLab's work. Physical and mental well-being affect people's ability to study, work, care for their families and participate in their communities.

LifeLab's health approach places emphasis on awareness, prevention, health literacy and improving people's ability to understand and access appropriate services. Community-based information can help individuals make informed decisions and recognize when professional care may be needed.

Health cannot be separated from the conditions in which people live. Nutrition, hygiene, safe water, education, employment, financial security and social support all influence well-being. LifeLab therefore connects health with its wider work rather than treating it as an isolated sector.

Areas of focus

  • Community health awareness.
  • Preventive health education.
  • Health literacy and access to reliable information.
  • Mental health and psychosocial awareness.
  • Nutrition and healthy living awareness.
  • Referral and navigation toward appropriate services.
  • Health considerations within education and livelihood programs.

Humanitarian Aid

Humanitarian assistance responds to situations in which people face urgent needs that threaten their health, safety or ability to maintain basic living conditions. Economic crises, displacement, conflict and other emergencies can rapidly increase household vulnerability.

LifeLab's humanitarian approach is centered on dignity and needs-based support. Assistance can take different forms depending on circumstances, available resources and the needs identified within affected communities.

Immediate assistance may include essential food and household items, hygiene supplies, seasonal support and other forms of material assistance. Where appropriate, cash or voucher approaches can provide households with greater flexibility in determining how best to meet essential needs.

Humanitarian support should also connect people to longer-term pathways whenever possible. Education, health, protection, skills development and livelihood opportunities can help families move from immediate assistance toward greater stability and resilience.

  • Emergency and rapid-response assistance.
  • Food and essential household support.
  • Non-food items and hygiene materials.
  • Seasonal and winter-related assistance.
  • Cash or voucher-based assistance where appropriate.
  • Referral to health, education and protection services.
  • Connections to livelihood and recovery opportunities.

Environment

Environmental conditions have a direct effect on human health, agriculture, livelihoods and quality of life. Resource scarcity, waste, water pressure and environmental degradation can create long-term challenges for communities and local economies.

LifeLab integrates environmental responsibility into community development by promoting awareness and practical approaches to resource management. Environmental action should help communities understand how everyday behavior, local production and economic activity affect the resources on which people depend.

Water is an especially important connection between the environment and LifeLab's other sectors. Efficient irrigation, responsible consumption, water conservation and improved awareness can contribute simultaneously to environmental sustainability, agriculture and household resilience.

Environmental engagement also provides opportunities for community and youth participation. Awareness campaigns, local initiatives, volunteering and practical environmental activities can encourage people to take greater responsibility for the places in which they live.

  • Environmental awareness and education.
  • Waste reduction and responsible waste practices.
  • Water conservation and sustainable water use.
  • Environmentally responsible agricultural practices.
  • Community environmental initiatives.
  • Tree planting and community greening activities.
  • Youth participation and environmental volunteering.
